## Answers

Three exercises about an n-gram language model where the lesson ships bigrams
and all three exercises say trigram. All run at **T0** — `code/main.py` imports
only `math`, `random` and `collections`.

### 1 — Local plausibility is one by construction

**ANSWER: every trigram and every bigram emitted was in the training data** —
**157 of 157** each, across 20 generated sentences. A trigram sampler draws only
from continuations it has counted, so "locally plausible" is 1.0000 and could not
have been anything else. The demo cannot fail at the half it is run to show.

**FINDING: 9 of the 20 outputs are training sentences reproduced word for word** —
**45%** of the "generation" is retrieval.

**MECHANISM: the incoherence is a splice at a shared bigram.** The eleven novel
sentences join two training sentences where they share a context, e.g.
`the dog ran after the cat sat on the mat .` On 1,000 Shakespeare sentences the
same 45% would be invisible.

### 2 — The direction of the result is a preprocessing choice

No Shakespeare corpus ships, so the corpus is 725 sentences from this phase's own
lesson documents, split 80/20, vocabulary 1683.

| test set | Laplace | Kneser-Ney | change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **open vocabulary** | 821.35 | **6763.22** | **−723.4%** |
| closed (22 sentences, all tokens seen) | 589.62 | **125.48** | **+78.7%** |
| unseen tokens mapped to a frequent one | 727.47 | 132.19 | +78.3% |

**ANSWER: the exercise's claim and its opposite come from the same two functions
on the same corpus.** The deciding step is out-of-vocabulary handling, which the
exercise never mentions.

**MECHANISM: KN has no unknown-word floor and Laplace is nothing but floor.** The
continuation term is `len(contexts[w]) / total_unique_bigrams`, zero for a word
never seen — and with a zero bigram count the whole probability is zero. **369 of
2042** held-out tokens are out of vocabulary (18.1%) and **289 positions**
(14.2%) get probability zero, clamped to 1e-12 by `perplexity`.

**FINDING: Laplace has its own defect, running the other way.** `laplace_prob`
divides by `unigrams[prev] + vocab_size` where it needs `prev`'s count *as a
context*. On a three-sentence corpus `</s>` occurs 3 times and is a context 0
times, so its distribution sums to **0.8235**. Kneser-Ney sums to exactly
**1.0** for every context tested — the model that loses by a factor of eight is
the correctly normalised one.

### 3 — The context the ranker needs is the context it lacks

The Birkbeck corpus is not here, so the 25 cases are generated by corrupting a
training word into a non-word that still has 3+ real neighbours — 3.68 candidates
each over a 40-word vocabulary of deliberately confusable words (cat/cot/cut,
hat/hit/had/hid, bat/ball, rat/ran). Without confusable words the candidate set
has one member and no ranker is consulted at all.

| ranker | overall | seen bigram (19) | unseen bigram (6) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| unigram frequency | 0.3200 | 7 | 1 |
| **bigram context (KN)** | **0.7200** | **16** | 2 |
| **edge** | — | **+0.4737** | **+0.1667** |

**MECHANISM: candidate generation never fails** — 25 of 25 recall, 0
single-candidate cases. All the error is in the ranking.

**MECHANISM: on an unseen context Kneser-Ney falls back to its continuation
term**, which ranks by how many *different* contexts a word has appeared in — a
unigram-like signal, and the frequency ranker already was one. The model speaks
where the correction was reachable and abstains where it was not.

**FINDING: so the back-off is the half of the system that handles the hard
cases.** 6 of 25 cases fall outside the model's evidence and it gets 2. That
back-off is what runs on the cases the headline number is not measuring.
